The challenge
Why a conversation, not a directory+
Choosing cosmetic surgery is one of the most personal, high-stakes decisions someone can make. The existing path is a maze of directories, unverifiable before-and-afters and opaque pricing, and it only adds to the fear. I interviewed 30+ patients and surgeons to map where trust breaks down. One finding became the product's spine: people don't want more options, they want to trust one.
My approach
The conversation is the product+
Oia replaces the marketplace with a concierge: an agent that searches, vets, compares and negotiates on the patient's behalf, so the person stays in a calm, one-to-one conversation. When the interface is a conversation, the copy is the design, not a layer added after the screens, so I scripted the dialogue first and built the UI to serve it. Oia's identity, voice, hard lines and worked examples live in a SOUL.md file I write and version like any other design artefact, so non-engineers can retrain her by editing text, not code. Her hard lines are as deliberate as anything on screen: she never gives medical advice, never promises an outcome, never fabricates a case, and never pushes a hesitant patient. I owned brand end to end too: name, wordmark, palette and voice. In a category that shouts, Oia had to feel like a quiet, expert friend, so I built Technical Luxury: a restrained interface of frosted glass over slow-moving gradients, calm enough for a decision this personal.
What I built
Live at heyoia.com+
SmartMatch shortlists surgeons by verified outcomes rather than who paid for placement, compared side by side in a mobile-first layout for late-night, one-handed use. Once a patient decides, Oia plans the whole trip: flights, hotel, surgery day, a recovery kit delivered ahead of arrival, and daily post-op check-ins, all inside the same conversation with the patient and the clinic's own coordinators, handed off to WhatsApp whenever that's easier for the patient. A Price Match layer negotiates the package across two or three clinics in parallel before it's ever shown to the patient. Onboarding is three plain-language questions, never a form, ending on the payoff of meeting Oia and seeing matches immediately. Oia is live at heyoia.com, shipped 0→1 and grounded in 30+ interviews and 3,500+ real cases reviewed with surgeons.

02

A video, not a form

Typing out a medical history takes time, and text alone can't show a surgeon what they need to see. So instead of a form, Oia asks for one short video: the patient explains what they want, in their own words, exactly as they would in a consultation. It's faster than typing, and once the patient consents, that same footage is shared privately with their surgeon at consultation, so the clinical team can judge feasibility from the real thing, not a written description.
